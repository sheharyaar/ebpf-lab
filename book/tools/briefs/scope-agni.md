The argument. The chapter before (src/proof.html, "One proof by hand, one bug by solver") proved one tnum operation by hand and by Z3, and found CVE-2021-3490 by encoding one kernel function by hand. Its own limits: one operation at a time, an encoding written by a person who may get it wrong (its "When it goes wrong" table), and two tidy-up invariants assumed rather than proved. Agni (Vishwanathan, Shachnai, Narayana, Nagarakatte, "Verifying the Verifier: eBPF Range Analysis Verification", CAV 2023; check title, authors, venue and year before you use them) removes the hand encoding: it takes the verifier's own C, compiles it to LLVM IR, translates that to SMT, and checks every ALU and branch operation of the range analysis, with the five abstract domains (the tnum and the four bounds) and the synchronisation between them, against a soundness specification, for many kernel versions; when a check fails it synthesises a concrete eBPF program that shows the bug. The reader leaves able to read the paper with its notation, to say what Agni proves and what it leaves out, and to judge what "the verifier is verified" can and cannot mean for a kernel they run.
