How to read this

Two paths. Straight through is the intended path. Every chapter follows one program, the maps/02-hash-map write counter you built in the playground, one step further: from C source to bytecode, into the kernel, past its checks, onto a hook, into a map, and back out to your loader. The second path is by topic. Every reference carries a tag (lineageISAloaderverifierruntimeHW), and the references at the back are grouped by it.

Words. A word in bold is being defined in that sentence or the next. After that it is used without ceremony, and it is in the glossary at the back, under the chapter that defined it.

Colours in the figures. Teal is your program and what the kernel does with it. Coral is the kernel's side of a boundary: helper functions, maps, and the point where the kernel refuses a program. Amber marks the one thing a figure wants you to watch. Grey is history, context and everything not in focus. Thin grey arrows are calls, hand-offs or "becomes"; thick coloured arrows follow your program or the data it moves.

The ribbon. The strip of dates under each chapter title is the history of eBPF by kernel version, drawn in full in the prologue. The dark cells are the span that chapter covers.

Check yourself. Every chapter ends with two to four questions. Answer out loud first, in full sentences, then turn to the answer key at the back, where the answers are in chapter order. A question you can half-answer is the one worth re-reading the chapter for. If you would rather be quizzed properly, hand this file to an agent running the Alvar probe skill and ask it to quiz you chapter by chapter: it asks through a real question tool, scores your reasoning rather than your letter, and writes what it found to .alvar/maps/.

Labs. Most chapters end with one lab, marked with its time and what it needs. Labs work on copies of your playground solutions in /tmp and never change the playground. Those that load a program need sudo. Each solution in the key says whether it was run, on which kernel and with which tools.

Line numbers. Every file and line of kernel source named in this book is from Linux v7.2, the kernel you run. They drift with every release; the chapter on the verifier's source shows how to find them again.

Printing. The pages you see are the pages that print. Print from Chrome with Margins: None, Headers and footers off and Background graphics on. Hints print in full, so cover them with a hand if you want to try first.
